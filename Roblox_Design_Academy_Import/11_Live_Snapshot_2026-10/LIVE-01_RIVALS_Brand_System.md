## Context
- Icon: a red faceless mannequin fist or glove on near-white.
- All 10 thumbnails follow the **same rule**: a bright red, featureless, outlined mannequin (the enemy or player), **one** stylized weapon, and a clean white, grey or lavender arena (sometimes sky blue).
- Several thumbnails are first-person views down a weapon at red targets.

## What Works
- **A single, ownable visual rule.** Red mannequin on white is recognisable from any thumbnail, at any size, even cropped. In the grid, nothing else looks like it.
- **Extreme figure-ground contrast.** Saturated red on desaturated light backgrounds. The focal point is never in doubt.
- **Variety inside the rule.** Every thumbnail features a different weapon or situation (knife, sniper, AK, minigun-like device, scope view), so the set shows *content breadth* without losing identity.
- **No text, no badges** (except a Halloween tag in the name). The image does all the work.
- The style **is the game's actual art direction** (minimal arena, flat-coloured characters). Honest.

## What Does Not Work
- Little sense of "who I am" or social play. Figures are anonymous by design. That fits an arena shooter, but it'd be wrong for social genres.

## Reusable Design Rules
1. Define **one visual rule** (subject treatment + background treatment + palette) and apply it to the icon and **every** thumbnail.
2. Vary *content* (weapon, map, mode) inside a fixed *style*.
3. One saturated hue on a neutral field is the cheapest strong identity there is.

## Anti-Patterns
- Copying "red figure on white" is copying RIVALS. Take the *principle* (one ownable rule), not the look.
