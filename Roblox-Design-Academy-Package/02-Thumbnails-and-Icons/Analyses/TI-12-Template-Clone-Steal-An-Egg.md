## Context
Both thumbnails share the same formula:
- a sunny desert ground and a blue sky
- a large dinosaur lying on the left with red "!!" alarm marks
- an avatar lifting a giant spiky egg
- a curved red arrow pointing at the egg
- "TREX EGG" in rainbow letters
- a green "$…/s" income number

The only difference is that Bike For Egg puts the avatar on a bicycle.

## What Works (the original formula)
- **Verb + stakes in one image**: you *steal* the *egg* from a *dangerous dinosaur*. The arrow and "!!" direct the eye, and the income number promises escalation.
- Bright, high-key palette and big shapes. It reads at grid size.

## What Does Not Work (the clone)
- **No distinct identity.** Next to the original in the grid, it reads as the same game or a knock-off. The added bike is the real hook, but it's a small detail in the frame.
- It inherits the original's text clutter (rainbow title + number + arrow + "!!").

## Reusable Design Rules
1. Borrow a **genre's grammar** (verb, stakes, escalation number) but **build the image around your own hook**. Here the bike should dominate the frame.
2. Test for originality: put your thumbnail next to the genre leader at 320×180. If a stranger can't tell which is which, redo it.

## Original application
Keep the genre grammar (verb, stakes, number) but frame your unique hook as the subject.

## Limitations
Success data (Bike For Egg is still on Up-and-Coming) shows that cloning can attract players short-term. This is **not** a claim that cloning fails. It's a claim that it gives up identity. See [AP-13](../../12-Anti-Patterns-and-Design-Critique/Anti-Pattern-Catalog.md).
