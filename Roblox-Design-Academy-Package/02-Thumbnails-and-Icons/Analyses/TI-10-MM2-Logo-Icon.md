## Context
- Icon: an abstract purple-and-orange knife shape that also forms a "2", on pure black. No character, no scene.
- Lead thumbnail: an in-game Halloween map scene with a green-eyed character in the centre. It's fairly busy and low-key.

## What Works
- **The icon is a logo**, the strongest kind of recognition for a game with an established audience. At 64 px it's unmistakable in the grid because it's the only abstract mark.
- Black background + two saturated hues = maximum separation.

## What Does Not Work
- A logo icon **explains nothing** to a new player. It works because MM2 is famous. For a new game it would be an [anti-pattern](../../12-Anti-Patterns-and-Design-Critique/Anti-Pattern-Catalog.md) (no verb, no genre).
- The lead thumbnail is cluttered (props, a house and a character) with no strong focal hierarchy.

## Reusable Design Rules
1. **Logo icons are for established brands.** New games need subject-and-genre icons, and can evolve toward a mark once recognised.
2. Even for brands, the *thumbnail* must still sell the situation.

## Original application
Only after a game is established, reduce the icon to its mark; before that keep subject + genre.
